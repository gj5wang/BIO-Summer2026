Introduction · 1/2

The Monopile Problem

Monopiles are commonly used as foundations for offshore wind turbines. Despite enabling the generation of large amounts of renewable energy, their presence disturbs the surrounding water flow, potentially affecting nearby physical and biological ocean processes. Monopiles can also alter water column stratification, which is the vertical layering that influences mixing, oxygen transport, and nutrient exchange.

Introduction · 2/2

My Tasks

My work estimates the impact that installing offshore wind farm monopiles has on Halifax Harbour. It consists of two studies. The first is a validation study that calculates drag, pressure, and wake data for flow past a cylinder. The second is an LES-based study that quantifies monopile-generated turbulence.

Study 1

Flow Past Cylinder Validation

Does the simulation reproduce known drag, shedding, and separation behaviour for flow past a cylinder?

Study 2

Turbulence Parameterization

How much turbulence does a monopile create, and what are αk and c₄, the two numbers that describe it?

Both studies simulate the same 8 m cylinder in cross-flow at Re = 4×10⁶, water depth 40 m, inflow velocity 0.5 m/s.

A standard k–ε ocean-model closure represents the unresolved monopile as a quadratic drag force; αk and c₄ are the two parameters this study estimates from LES.

Results · 5/9

Ocean-Model Parameters

The effective drag coefficient sets the wake's available drag power; αk is the fraction of available drag power contributing to wake TKE production. c₄ then links resolved dissipation back to the source term, giving Spilek and Spileε. These are the two terms a regional ocean model plugs in directly as unresolved monopile turbulence.

Conclusion

Summary

The validation study confirms the simulation reproduces published drag, pressure, shedding, and separation behaviour for post-critical cylinder flow (Re = 4×10⁶).

The LES-based analysis estimates αk = 0.445 and c₄ = 1.478, indicating that wake TKE production corresponds to approximately 44.5% of the available drag power, below the common assumption of αk = 1.

Together, these provide an LES-informed parameterization for implementation in a regional ocean model's k–ε closure: Spilek and Spileε.

Key Limitations

SGS kinetic energy is currently neglected in k but included in ε; c₄ assumes local equilibrium in the near wake; offline calibration against the model is still pending, and can give more accurate results.
